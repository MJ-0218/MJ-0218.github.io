Imported US chips, cookies and granola bars are easy to order online now.
If someone at home has a food allergy, it's worth knowing how American labels work. Here's what the US FDA says.

![US snack label card](/assets/cards/ph-imported-snacks-allergen-label-contains-summary.png)

## The 9 major allergens on US labels
US law names these as major food allergens:
- Milk, eggs, fish
- Crustacean shellfish (like crab, lobster, shrimp)
- Tree nuts (like almonds, walnuts)
- Peanuts, wheat, soybeans
- **Sesame**, added as the 9th, effective January 1, 2023

## Where to find them
FDA says the allergen has to appear in one of two ways:
1. **In brackets** after the ingredient, like "whey (milk)" or "lecithin (soy)".
2. In a **"Contains" line** near the ingredient list, like "Contains Wheat, Milk, and Soy."

Sometimes the ingredient name already says it, like buttermilk, so you won't see brackets there.

## One catch
FDA notes you may still find products that **don't list sesame** as an allergen, especially older stock.
So if sesame is your concern, read the full ingredient list too.

These are US label rules. Products made for other markets don't always follow them, so check the label on the item you receive.
