Beach outing, swimming, or just rinsing your lens case at the sink?
If you wear contacts, here's what the US FDA says about water.

![Contact lenses and water card](/assets/cards/ph-contact-lenses-beach-pool-water-summary.png)

## Keep lenses away from all water
FDA says not to expose contact lenses to **any water**: tap, bottled, distilled, lake or **ocean** water.
Water exposure has been linked to **Acanthamoeba keratitis**, a serious eye infection of the cornea.

FDA also says never to use non-sterile water, including homemade saline.

## Everyday lens habits
- **Wash your hands** before touching your lenses.
- **Rub and rinse** your lenses as your eye doctor directs.
- **Don't top off** old solution in your case. Throw it out after each use.
- Don't put lenses in your mouth to wet them. Saliva isn't sterile.

## Your lens case
Rinse it with fresh multi-purpose solution, then let it **air-dry** every time you take your lenses out.
Replace the case **every 3 months**, or as your eye doctor says.

## When to stop
If your eyes turn **red, irritated**, or your vision changes, take the lenses out right away and see your eye doctor.
