Hi, mga ka-Humid!
Baon is life, especially when everything outside costs double.
But in our heat, that adobo in your bag has a timer. Here's how long, based on the US CDC.

![Baon food safety card](/assets/cards/ph-baon-food-safety-1-hour-rule-summary.png)

## The 2-hour and 1-hour rule
CDC says never leave perishable food out for **more than 2 hours**.
If it's sitting somewhere **above 90°F (about 32°C)**, like a hot car or a picnic, the limit drops to **1 hour**.

Let's be honest: a lot of our afternoons are above 32°C.

## Why the timer matters
Bacteria multiply fast in what CDC calls the **"Danger Zone," between 40°F and 140°F** (about 4°C to 60°C).
Room temperature in Manila sits right in the middle of that.

## How to pack it smarter
- Pack food into **shallow containers** before chilling, so it cools quickly. CDC recommends several small, shallow ones over one big tub.
- Keep the baon with an ice pack until lunch if there's no ref at work or school.
- Don't leave the lunch bag in a parked car.

## Reheating and leftovers
- Reheat leftovers until steaming hot. CDC's target is **165°F (about 74°C)**.
- A food thermometer is the only sure way to check, says CDC.
- Thaw frozen ulam in the ref, cold water or microwave, not on the counter.

Busog and safe, that's the goal. Ingat palagi!
