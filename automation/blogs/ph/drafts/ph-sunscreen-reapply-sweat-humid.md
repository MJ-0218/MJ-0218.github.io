Hi mga ka-Humid!
Commute, errands, a quick walk to the sari-sari store… and suddenly your sunscreen has melted off.
Sound familiar?

Let's fix the habit, not just the product.
These tips come from the US FDA's sunscreen guide, which explains what the labels actually mean.

![Sunscreen reapply card](/assets/cards/ph-sunscreen-reapply-sweat-humid-summary.png)

## How often should you reapply?

FDA's advice is simple: **reapply at least every 2 hours**, and more often if you're sweating or swimming.
In our weather, "sweating" is basically all day, so keep a small bottle in your bag.

Put it on **15 minutes before** you head out, too.
FDA says this gives the sunscreen enough time to work.

## "Water resistant" is not "waterproof"

Here's the one that surprises people.
FDA says there's **no such thing as waterproof sunscreen**. All sunscreens eventually wash off.

Products labelled "water resistant" must state whether they stay effective for **40 minutes or 80 minutes** when you're swimming or sweating.
So check that number on the label, and reapply when the time is up.

## What to look for on the label

- **Broad spectrum**: protects against both UVA and UVB.
- **SPF 15 or higher**: FDA's minimum suggestion for regular use.
- **Water resistant (40 or 80 minutes)**: useful if you sweat a lot or swim.

One more myth to drop: SPF is not a timer.
FDA explains that SPF 15 doesn't mean you can stay out 15 times longer. It's about the amount of sun, not the time.

## Spots people forget

FDA lists the usual misses: ears, nose, lips, back of the neck, hands, tops of feet and along the hairline.
An SPF lip balm takes care of the lips, sis.

## Keep your bottle cool

Leaving sunscreen in a hot car or in direct sun isn't great for it.
FDA suggests wrapping the bottle in a towel or keeping it in the shade when you're out in the heat.

Stay protected, and don't forget that 2-hour reminder!
