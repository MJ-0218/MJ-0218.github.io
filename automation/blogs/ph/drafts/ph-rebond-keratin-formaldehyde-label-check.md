Hi, mga ka-Humid!
Frizz season never really ends here, so rebond and keratin treatments stay popular.
Before you book, here's what the US FDA says about some hair smoothing products.

![Hair smoothing label card](/assets/cards/ph-rebond-keratin-formaldehyde-label-check-summary.png)

## Why heat matters
Many smoothing treatments are applied to the hair, then sealed in with heat.
FDA says these products often contain **formaldehyde**, also called **formalin** or **methylene glycol**.

When the solution is heated, the formaldehyde is **released into the air as a gas**.
If the salon isn't properly ventilated, both the stylist and the client can breathe it in.

## The 3 words to look for
If you're buying a product yourself, FDA says to check the ingredient list for:

- **Formaldehyde**
- **Formalin**
- **Methylene glycol**

Retail products are required to list ingredients.
If a product has **no ingredient list**, FDA recommends you **don't buy it**.

## At the salon
Ask your stylist if the product has formaldehyde-related ingredients.
Salon-only products may not carry an ingredient list, so asking is the easiest way to know.

If they aren't sure, FDA says you may not want to use it, especially if you've had a reaction before.

## Reactions people have reported
FDA lists eye irritation, headaches and dizziness, sore throat, cough, wheezing, nausea, chest pain, vomiting and rash.
The longer and stronger the exposure, the higher the potential risk.

If something feels off, tell your doctor and report it to FDA.
