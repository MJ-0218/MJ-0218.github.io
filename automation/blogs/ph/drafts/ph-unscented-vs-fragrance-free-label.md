If strong scents give you a headache or your skin gets itchy easily, this one's for you.
Two words on the bottle look the same but mean different things: **unscented** and **fragrance-free**.

![Unscented vs fragrance-free card](/assets/cards/ph-unscented-vs-fragrance-free-label-summary.png)

## "Unscented" can still contain fragrance
Surprise! The US FDA says even some products labeled "unscented" may have fragrance ingredients.
A maker might add just enough to cover the smell of other ingredients, so you don't notice any scent.

## Why the list just says "Fragrance"
In the US, a cosmetic sold in stores or online must list its ingredients.
But fragrance and flavor can be listed simply as **"Fragrance"** or **"Flavor"**, because the formulas count as trade secrets.

So one word on the label can stand for a whole mix of ingredients.

## Where fragrance hides
It isn't only perfume and cologne.
FDA notes fragrance is common in shampoo, shower gel, shaving cream and body lotion.

## If your skin is sensitive
Some people are allergic or sensitive to certain ingredients, even ones that are fine for most people.
FDA's suggestion: choose **fragrance-free** products and check the ingredient list carefully.

You can also message the brand directly if something on the list isn't clear.

## Quick shopping checklist
- Look for "fragrance-free", not just "unscented".
- Scan the list for "Fragrance", "Parfum" or "Flavor".
- Buying online? Zoom in on the ingredient photo before checking out.
