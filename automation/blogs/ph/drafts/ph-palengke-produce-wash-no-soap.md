Hi, mga ka-Humid!
Fresh from the palengke, your bayong is full of gulay and prutas.
Before you cook or slice, here's how the US FDA says to wash them.

![Washing produce card](/assets/cards/ph-palengke-produce-wash-no-soap-summary.png)

## At the market
Pick produce that **isn't bruised or damaged**.
For pre-cut fruit like sliced melon, FDA says to choose items that are **refrigerated or surrounded by ice**.

## At home: start with your hands
Wash your hands for at least **20 seconds** with soap and warm water, before and after you prepare produce.

## Rinse under running water
Wash all produce **under running water** before eating or cooking, even the ones from your own garden.
Gently rub it while you rinse.

## Skip the soap
FDA does **not** recommend washing fruits and vegetables with soap, detergent or commercial produce wash.
Produce is porous, so soap can be absorbed even after rinsing and can make you sick.

## Scrub, then dry
- Use a clean brush on **firm produce** like melons and cucumbers.
- Dry with a **clean cloth or paper towel** to reduce bacteria on the surface even more.
- Cut away **bruised or damaged** parts, and throw out anything that looks rotten.

## Even if you'll peel it
Wash it first.
FDA explains that dirt and bacteria on the skin can get onto the part you eat when you peel or cut.

Washing reduces bacteria but doesn't remove all of it, and cooking kills harmful bacteria.
Enjoy your fresh finds, mga ka-Humid!
