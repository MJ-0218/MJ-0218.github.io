Green packaging, leaf logos, the word **organic** in big letters... it's tempting, right?
Here's what that word actually means for skincare, based on the US FDA.

![Organic skincare card](/assets/cards/ph-organic-skincare-label-what-it-means-summary.png)

## FDA doesn't define "organic"
In the US, the laws FDA uses for cosmetics don't define the word "organic" at all.
So FDA itself isn't the one checking that claim.

## Who does?
The US Department of Agriculture runs the **National Organic Program**.
It defines "organic", certifies farm ingredients, and sets label rules based on the **percentage** of organic ingredients.

An organic-labeled cosmetic still has to follow FDA's own labeling and safety rules too.

## Organic doesn't mean safer
This is the big one.
FDA says an ingredient's source doesn't decide whether it's safe.

Plenty of plants, organic or not, contain substances that can be toxic or trigger allergies.
Every cosmetic, organic or not, has to meet the same safety requirement.

## How to shop smarter
- Look past the leaf logo and read the full ingredient list.
- Sensitive skin? Patch-test new products, even "natural" ones.
- If a product claims organic certification, check which program certified it.

Take care, and happy label-reading!
