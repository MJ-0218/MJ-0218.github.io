Hi mga ka-Humid!
Halloween parties are coming, and a pair of cat-eye or vampire lenses can finish a costume perfectly.
Before you pop them in, here's what the US FDA wants you to know.

![Colored contacts card](/assets/cards/ph-halloween-colored-contact-lens-safety-summary.png)

## They're not just accessories

FDA says decorative contact lenses are **medical devices**, just like lenses that correct your vision.
In the US, that means you need a prescription even for colored or costume lenses.

Rules can be different in the Philippines, so ask your eye doctor what applies here.
The safety advice below works anywhere, though.

## What can go wrong

FDA lists the risks of lenses that are obtained without a proper fit or used incorrectly:

- A scratch on the top layer of your eye (corneal abrasion)
- Allergic reactions like itchy, watery, red eyes
- Decreased vision
- Infection
- Blindness

## The do's

- **Get an eye exam** so the lenses fit your eyes. A wrong fit can damage them.
- **Follow the cleaning and wearing instructions** that come with the lenses.
- **Take them out and see an eye doctor right away** if your eyes are red, keep hurting or have discharge.

## The don'ts

- **Don't share lenses**, not even with your barkada. FDA compares it to sharing a toothbrush.
- **Don't buy from street vendors, novelty shops or unknown online sellers**, FDA says, since lenses from these places may be contaminated or counterfeit.

## Signs to watch for

Redness, eye pain that doesn't go away, or blurry vision can mean an infection.
FDA warns an untreated eye infection can become serious.
