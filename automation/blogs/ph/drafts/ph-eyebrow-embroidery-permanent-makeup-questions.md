Tired of drawing your brows every morning? Eyebrow embroidery and other permanent makeup sound like a dream.
Before you book, here's what the US FDA says, plus a few questions worth asking the salon.

![Permanent makeup card](/assets/cards/ph-eyebrow-embroidery-permanent-makeup-questions-summary.png)

## It's a tattoo, plain and simple
FDA puts it directly: permanent makeup is a type of tattoo.
A needle puts colored ink into your skin so it looks like brows, eyeliner or lip liner.

And tattoos last a lifetime, so think of it as a long-term decision.

## About the inks
More than 50 pigments and shades are in use, FDA notes.
Some color additives are approved for cosmetics, but **none are approved for injection into the skin**.

Some pigments are even industrial grade, the kind used for printer ink or car paint.

## Risks FDA lists
Tattoos can cause side effects, and some can't be undone. FDA's list includes:

- Infections
- Allergic reactions
- Granulomas and keloids
- More sunburn, redness or scarring
- Swelling and burning
- MRI complications
- Problems with removal

## Questions to ask the salon
- Are the needles new and opened in front of me?
- What ink brand do you use, and can I see the label?
- What aftercare do you recommend, and who do I call if it gets red or swollen?

In the US, tattoo shop practices are usually regulated by local governments, so check your city's rules too.

## Mention it at your next MRI
Since MRI complications are on FDA's list, tell the technician you have permanent makeup.
