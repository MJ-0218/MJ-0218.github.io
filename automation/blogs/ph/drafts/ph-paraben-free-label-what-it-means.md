Hi, mga ka-beauty!
"Paraben-free" is printed on so many bottles now. But what are parabens, and why are they there in the first place?
Here's a quick, no-panic guide based on the US FDA.

![Parabens card](/assets/cards/ph-paraben-free-label-what-it-means-summary.png)

## What parabens are for
Parabens are a family of chemicals used as **preservatives** in cosmetics.
Their job: stop harmful **bacteria and mold** from growing, protecting both the product and you.

That matters a lot in our humid weather, where opened jars sit in warm bathrooms.

## How to spot them
They're easy to find because the name usually ends in "paraben". The most common ones are:

- Methylparaben
- Propylparaben
- Butylparaben
- Ethylparaben

Labels often list more than one, sometimes together with other preservatives.

## Where you'll see them
FDA says parabens can be in makeup, moisturisers, hair care and shaving products.
Many big deodorant brands currently don't use them, though some might.

## Are they a problem?
FDA scientists keep reviewing studies.
For now, the agency says it has no information showing parabens, as used in cosmetics, affect human health.

FDA is also asking a fair question: if parabens are dropped, are there safer preservatives to replace them?

## So, paraben-free or not?
It's your choice, and the ingredient list gives you what you need to decide.
Just remember a water-based product still needs *some* way to stay free of mold.

- Check the list for "-paraben" names if you prefer to avoid them.
- Note the "use within" period after opening.
- Keep jars closed and away from the shower spray.

Take care, and may your jars stay mold-free!
