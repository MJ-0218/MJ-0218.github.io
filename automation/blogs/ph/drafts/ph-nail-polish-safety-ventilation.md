Hi mga ka-Humid!
Doing your own nails at home saves money, and it's fun with a K-drama on.
But nail polish and remover need a bit of care, especially in small rooms.

Here are the basics from the US FDA's nail product guide.

![DIY mani card](/assets/cards/ph-nail-polish-safety-ventilation-summary.png)

## Read the label first

FDA says consumers should read nail product labels carefully and follow any warnings.
Some ingredients can be harmful if swallowed, even though they're fine on your nails.

So keep bottles away from little kids and pets, and never leave remover in a drinking glass.

## Open a window

FDA's tip is simple: **make sure you have good ventilation** when you use nail products.
In a closed bedroom with the aircon on, open a window or move near one while you paint.

## Watch out for flames and heat

FDA warns that some nail products can easily catch fire near the flame of a stove's pilot light, a lit cigarette, or a heat source like a curling iron.
So don't do your nails in the kitchen while cooking, and keep the curling iron unplugged nearby.

## Protect your eyes

Nail products can be dangerous if they get in your eyes, FDA says.
Close bottles right after use and wipe spills straight away.

## Reactions happen

Infections and allergic reactions can occur with some nail products.
If your skin around the nail turns red, itchy or swollen, stop using the product and see a doctor if it doesn't settle.

## Buying polish and remover

Since retail cosmetics must list their ingredients, compare labels if a product bothered you before.
Smaller bottles make sense if you only do your nails once in a while.

Enjoy your mani, sis!
