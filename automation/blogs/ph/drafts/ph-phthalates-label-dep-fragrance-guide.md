"Phthalate-free" is a label we see more and more.
So what are phthalates, and how do you check for them? Here's the US FDA's explanation.

![Phthalates label card](/assets/cards/ph-phthalates-label-dep-fragrance-guide-summary.png)

## What they are
Phthalates are chemicals used to make plastics more durable.
In beauty products, they also work as **solvents and stabilizers in perfumes and fragrances**.

## Where you may find them
The FDA lists **nail polishes, hair sprays, aftershave lotions, cleansers and shampoos**.

## The three names to know
- **DBP** (dibutylphthalate): used in nail polish to reduce cracking
- **DMP** (dimethylphthalate): used in hair spray for a flexible hold
- **DEP** (diethylphthalate): used as a solvent and fixative in fragrance

According to the FDA's 2010 survey, DBP and DMP are used rarely.
**DEP** appears to be the only one still commonly used.

## The "fragrance" catch
Ingredient lists don't have to break down the parts of a **fragrance or flavor**.
So phthalates inside a fragrance may not show on the label.

## What the FDA says
Based on current safety information, the FDA **does not have safety concerns** with DEP as currently used in cosmetics and fragrances.
If you'd still rather avoid it, the FDA suggests choosing products **without "Fragrance" or "Flavor"** in the ingredient list.
