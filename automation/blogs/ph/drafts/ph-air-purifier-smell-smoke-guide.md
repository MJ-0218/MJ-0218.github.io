Hi mga ka-Humid!
Fried fish for lunch, and the smell is still in the sala by dinner?
Before buying an air purifier to fix that, it helps to know what it can and can't do.

Here's the simple version of the US EPA's air cleaner guide.

![Air purifier filter types card](/assets/cards/ph-air-purifier-smell-smoke-guide-summary.png)

## Step one isn't buying anything

EPA says the most effective ways to improve indoor air are to **reduce or remove the source** and **ventilate with clean outdoor air**.
Filtration works as a supplement to those.

So open the windows and use the range hood while cooking first.

## Two jobs, two kinds of filters

EPA explains that most filters are designed to filter **either particles or gases**.

- **Particles** (smoke, dust, PM2.5): look for a high **CADR** and a **HEPA** filter. EPA notes many portable air cleaners reach a high CADR with HEPA.
- **Gases and odors**: look for an **activated carbon** filter or another filter made for gases. EPA says these can work if there's a large amount of carbon material in the filter.

Many units have both. Some only have one, usually for particles.

## A catch about the numbers

The CADR rating is for **particles only**, EPA says.
There's no widely used rating system for how well a purifier removes gases, so check the product details carefully.

## Avoid ozone makers

EPA advises avoiding air cleaners that intentionally produce ozone, since ozone irritates the lungs.

## Plan for filter changes

EPA says every filter needs regular replacement, because a clogged one stops doing its job.
Check that replacement filters are easy to buy before you choose a model.
