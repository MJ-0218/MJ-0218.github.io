Hi, mga ka-Humid!
Typhoon season means brownouts, and brownouts mean one big question: is the food in the ref still okay?
Here's the US FDA's simple guide.

![Brownout fridge card](/assets/cards/ph-brownout-fridge-food-safety-4-hours-summary.png)

## Rule one: keep the doors closed
When the power goes out, FDA says to keep the fridge and freezer doors **closed as much as possible**.
Every peek lets cold air out.

## How long does the cold last?
- **Fridge**: about **4 hours** if it stays unopened.
- **Full freezer**: about **48 hours** if the door stays closed.

So a packed freezer holds up much better than a half-empty one.

## When the power comes back
Check the freezer first.
If food still has **ice crystals** or is at **4°C or below**, FDA says it's safe to refreeze or cook.

For the fridge, food should be fine if the outage was **4 hours or less** and the door stayed shut.
Throw out perishables like meat, fish, milk, eggs and leftovers that sat **above 4°C for 4 hours or more**.

## Don't trust the sniff test
FDA is clear: **you can't rely on appearance or odor**.
Food can make you sick even when it looks and smells fine.

## Be ready before the next storm
- Put a small **fridge thermometer** inside, and one in the freezer. FDA calls them the best way to know the real temperature, and they're usually cheap.
- Keep the fridge at **4°C or below** and the freezer at **-18°C**.
- Freeze a few bottles of water now. They keep the freezer full and colder for longer.

Stay safe this typhoon season, mga ka-Humid!
