Hi, mga ka-beauty!
Pampaputi creams with "rejuv" and "glass skin" promises are all over our feeds.
Before you message that seller, here's a quick label check from the US FDA.

![Mercury label check card](/assets/cards/ph-whitening-cream-mercury-label-words-summary.png)

## Why this matters
The US FDA says to **avoid skin creams, beauty soaps and lotions that contain mercury**.
Products like these are usually sold as skin lighteners and anti-aging treatments that promise to remove age spots, freckles, blemishes and wrinkles.

FDA also notes that teens may use them as acne treatments.
So if you have a younger sister or cousin into skincare, share this with her too.

## The 5 words to look for
Flip the jar or box and read the ingredient list.
If you see any of these, FDA says mercury is in it and you should **stop using the product immediately**:

1. **Mercurous chloride**
2. **Calomel**
3. **Mercuric**
4. **Mercurio**
5. **Mercury**

## Red flags before you add to cart
- **No label, no ingredient list.** FDA's advice: don't assume it's fine.
- **"Rebranded" or repacked jars** with a sticker and nothing else. Same problem, no way to check.
- **Sold only through DMs, live selling or chat apps.** FDA says these products are often promoted on social media and sold through mobile apps.

## Already using one? Do this
- Stop using it.
- Wash your hands and any skin that touched it thoroughly.
- Talk to your doctor or a clinic, especially if you notice tremors, tingling or numbness in your hands, feet or around the mouth.
- Before throwing it out, **seal it in a plastic bag or leak-proof container**, then ask your LGU or barangay about how to dispose of it.

## How to shop smarter
When you compare brightening products, start with the ingredient list, not the before-and-after photos.
A clear label, a full ingredient list and a seller you can trace are the basics.
