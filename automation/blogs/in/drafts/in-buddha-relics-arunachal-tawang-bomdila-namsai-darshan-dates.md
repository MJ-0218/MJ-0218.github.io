Namaste everyone!
If you've ever wanted to visit Tawang, this might be the most special time of the year to go.

The **Holy Relics of Lord Buddha** are coming to **Arunachal Pradesh** for public darshan from **31 October to 21 November 2026**.
They'll travel to three places: **Tawang, Bomdila and Namsai**.

![Buddha relics Arunachal card](/assets/cards/in-buddha-relics-arunachal-tawang-bomdila-namsai-darshan-dates-summary.png)

## The darshan schedule
Here's where the relics will be, and when.

| Dates | Venue | What's happening |
|---|---|---|
| 31 Oct | Tawang Monastery (Gaden Namgyal Lhatse) | Arrival and inaugural ceremony |
| 1–7 Nov | Tawang Monastery | Public darshan and exposition |
| 8–13 Nov | TGL Monastery (Lower Gompa), Bomdila | Public darshan and exposition |
| 14–20 Nov | Golden Pagoda, Namsai | Public darshan and exposition |
| 21 Nov | Namsai to New Delhi | Ceremonial departure |

So if you're only free on a weekend, check which town matches your dates first.

## Opening days at Tawang
The inauguration at Tawang Monastery is set for **10:00 AM to 11:30 AM on 31 October**.
Union Culture and Tourism Minister **Gajendra Singh Shekhawat** is scheduled to attend.

Another opening event, along with the exhibition, is planned for **1 November**.
Chief Minister **Pema Khandu** is the chief guest.

That day also falls on **Lhabab Düchen**, a Tibetan Buddhist festival marking the Buddha's descent from the heavenly realm.
Pretty beautiful timing, right?

## Opening ceremonies at Bomdila and Namsai
At Bomdila's Lower Gompa, the inaugural ceremony on **8 November** is scheduled for **3:00 PM to 4:00 PM**.
At the Golden Pagoda in Namsai, it's **11:00 AM to 12 noon on 14 November**.

The Ministry says timings and movement can change because of **weather, security and operational** reasons.
In the hills, that's worth keeping in mind before you book anything.

## Where these relics come from
The relics come from **Piprahwa** in Siddharthnagar district, Uttar Pradesh.
It's a site linked to ancient Kapilavastu and the Shakya clan of the Buddha.

The Archaeological Survey of India excavated there between **1971 and 1977** and recovered **22** sacred relics in all.
Of these, **20** are kept at the National Museum in New Delhi and **two** at the Indian Museum, Kolkata.

## More to see while you're there
The International Buddhist Confederation is putting up an exhibition, **"Unearthing the Sacred Piprahwa"**, at all three towns.
It tells the story of the relics and how they were found.

There's also an international conference at **Kalawangpo Convention Hall, Tawang, on 2 November**.
Monks and scholars from several countries are expected.

## Before you go
Arunachal is hill country, and plans there can shift at short notice.
Keep a buffer day, and check for updates from the state government or the Ministry of Culture before you travel.

Wishing you a peaceful darshan!
