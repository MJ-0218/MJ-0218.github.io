If you or your family are in the cyclone-hit areas, this is a quick, useful one.

After **Cyclone Arnab** hit the coast of **Andhra Pradesh and Odisha**, heavy rain, flash floods, fallen trees and power cuts disrupted mobile networks in parts of both states.
The **Department of Telecommunications (DoT)** says restoration is moving fast, and there's a way to stay connected even if your own operator is down.

![Cyclone Arnab ICR card](/assets/cards/in-cyclone-arnab-no-network-icr-how-to-connect-summary.png)

## What is ICR?
**Intra-Circle Roaming (ICR)** lets your phone connect to **another operator's network** in the same telecom circle.
So if your SIM's operator has no coverage where you are, you can still make calls and use mobile services through another one.

During disasters, DoT tells operators to switch ICR on.
It's active across the affected districts right now, **whichever operator you use**.

## How to connect: two steps
1. Keep **"Automatic Network Selection"** turned on in your phone settings.
2. If it doesn't connect automatically, go to **Settings > Mobile Networks > Network Operators** and **manually select** another available network.

That's it.
Share this with family members who may not know about it.

## Network status: Andhra Pradesh
- Overall telecom network availability: **99.18%**
- West Godavari: **98.5%**
- Dr. B. R. Ambedkar Konaseema: **91.96%**

Power cuts and **optical fibre cable cuts** are the main reasons for the remaining gaps.
**11 Cell on Wheels (COWs)**, which are mobile towers on vehicles, have been deployed to strengthen emergency communication.

## Network status: Odisha
In **Ganjam, Gajapati and Kandhamal**, things are improving as the rain eases and floodwaters recede.
Operators have started restoration work at affected sites.

## Where to get updates
DoT posts updates on its official handles, including X (**@DoT_India**).

Please stay safe, keep your phone charged where you can, and check on your neighbours.
