Hello hello!
When the haze rolls in, air purifier ads suddenly appear everywhere.
Before you grab the one on sale, here's a checklist based on the US EPA's air cleaner guide.

![Air purifier checklist card](/assets/cards/my-air-purifier-buying-guide-haze-summary.png)

## 1. Start with CADR

EPA says to choose a purifier whose **CADR is large enough for the room**.
A higher CADR filters more particles and covers a bigger area.

Quick sizing from EPA's chart (for an 8-foot ceiling):

| Room area | Minimum CADR |
|---|---|
| 100 sq ft | 65 cfm |
| 200 sq ft | 130 cfm |
| 300 sq ft | 195 cfm |
| 400 sq ft | 260 cfm |

Measure length × width of the room, then compare with the room size on the box.

## 2. HEPA for fine particles

EPA notes that portable air cleaners often reach a high CADR by using a **HEPA filter**.
For smoke particles, that's the spec to look for.

## 3. Watch out for ozone

EPA says to avoid air cleaners that intentionally produce ozone, since ozone is a lung irritant.
Some ionizers and similar types may emit ozone too, so read the product details carefully.

## 4. Budget for filters

All filters need regular replacement, EPA says. A clogged filter won't work well.
Check the replacement filter price and whether it's easy to find in Malaysia before you buy, lah.

## 5. Run it right

More air passes through the filter at higher fan speeds, and running it longer filters more air, EPA explains.
Pick a model that's quiet enough at the speed you'll actually use.
