Hello hello!
Maybe a friend gave you a "homemade" lightening cream, or you bought a jar with no label from an online shop.
Don't panic, here's a calm checklist based on the US FDA.

![Step-by-step card](/assets/cards/my-skin-lightening-cream-mercury-what-to-do-summary.png)

## Step 1: Check the label (if there is one)
FDA says that if any of these words appear, the product contains mercury:
**mercurous chloride, calomel, mercuric, mercurio, mercury**.

No label or no ingredient list?
FDA's advice is simple: don't assume it's fine.

## Step 2: Stop and wash
If you find a mercury word, or you can't check at all, stop using it.
FDA says to **thoroughly wash your hands** and any other part of your body that touched the product.

## Step 3: Watch for these signs
FDA lists these as signs and symptoms of mercury poisoning:

- Irritability or shyness
- Tremors
- Changes in vision or hearing
- Memory problems or depression
- Numbness and tingling in the hands, feet or around the mouth

If you notice any of these, or you're pregnant or breastfeeding, see a doctor or klinik and bring the product along.

## Step 4: Throw it out the right way
Before throwing it away, **seal it in a plastic bag or a leak-proof container**.
FDA also suggests checking with your local authority on how to dispose of it, so ask your local council.

## Step 5: Shop smarter next time
These creams are usually sold as skin lighteners or anti-aging treatments, and are often promoted on social media and sold through apps.
When you compare brightening products, start with a clear label and a full ingredient list.

Take it slow, and look after your skin the safe way!
