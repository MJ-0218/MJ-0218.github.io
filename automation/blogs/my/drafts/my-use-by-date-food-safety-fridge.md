Hello hello!
Ever stared at a yoghurt one day past its date and wondered if it's still okay?
Here's what the US FDA says about dates and spoilage.

![Use by card](/assets/cards/my-use-by-date-food-safety-fridge-summary.png)

## What "use by" actually means
FDA explains that a "use by" date is the maker's recommendation for **best flavour or quality**.
In FDA's words, **the date is not a food safety date**.

After it, the taste, colour or texture may change, but the food may still be safe.
If you're unsure, or it looks questionable, throw it out.

## The big exception: baby food
**Infant formula and some baby foods** must be used by the use-by date on the pack.
No stretching it, ever.

## Signs to bin it straight away
- **Mould**: FDA says the safest practice is to discard mouldy food.
- **Sticky or bulging cans**: stickiness outside a can may mean a leak.
- Anything that looks or smells off.

But don't rely on smell alone.
FDA warns food can make you very sick **even when it doesn't look, smell or taste spoiled**.

## Freezer burn is not dangerous
Those grey-brown dry patches on frozen meat are **a quality issue, not a safety issue**.
Wrap food airtight next time to avoid them.

## Quick kitchen habits
- Keep the fridge at **4°C or below**.
- Store dry food away from cleaning products and chemicals.

Less waste, safer meals. Selamat memasak!
