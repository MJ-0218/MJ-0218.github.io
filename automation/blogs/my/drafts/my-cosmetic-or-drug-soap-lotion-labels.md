Hello hello!
Ever noticed some lotions have a "Drug Facts" box and others don't?
The US FDA sorts bathroom products by what they claim to do. Here's the quick version.

![Cosmetic or drug card](/assets/cards/my-cosmetic-or-drug-soap-lotion-labels-summary.png)

## Making you look nice = cosmetic
A lotion meant to make you more attractive is a cosmetic, FDA says.
Think plain moisturiser or a scented body lotion.

## Claiming to treat something = drug
Once a product is meant to treat or prevent disease, or to change how the body works, it becomes a drug.
FDA's examples are worth knowing:

- Soaps and cleansers sold as **"antibacterial"**
- **Sunscreen** products
- Skin protectants and skin bleaches
- Treatments for **acne, eczema or rosacea**

## Some are both
A lotion that moisturises **and** protects you from the sun is both a cosmetic and a drug.
It has to meet the rules for both categories.

## Why this helps you shop
Words like "antibacterial" or an acne-treatment claim are bigger promises than "smells nice" or "feels soft".
When a label makes a treatment claim, look for the active ingredient and directions, and follow them.

## Quick label checklist
- Sunscreen: check the directions for how much and how often to apply.
- "Antibacterial": look for the active ingredient listed on the pack.
- Skin condition flaring up? A doctor's advice beats a new cream.

Happy shopping, and jom read the label first!
