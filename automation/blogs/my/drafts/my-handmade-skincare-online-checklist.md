Hello hello!
Handmade soaps, balms and body butters make lovely gifts, especially with year-end sales coming.
Before you check out from a small seller, here's a quick label checklist built from the US FDA's guide for home-based cosmetic makers.

![Handmade skincare checklist card](/assets/cards/my-handmade-skincare-online-checklist-summary.png)

## 1. Home-made is fine, but safety still counts
In the US, it isn't against the law to make cosmetics at home, FDA says.
But the maker is responsible for producing them somewhere that won't contaminate them.

Cosmetics don't have to be sterile, yet **microbial contamination** can be a health hazard.

## 2. "Natural" isn't a defined term
FDA hasn't defined "natural" for cosmetic labels, and it has no rules for "organic" either.
Choosing natural or organic ingredients is **no guarantee they're safe**, FDA adds.

## 3. Ingredients by their real names
Good labels list each ingredient by its common or usual name.
FDA tells makers not to tuck words like "natural" into the ingredient list itself.

## 4. A real business address
Under US rules, a P.O. box or a website address isn't enough on the label.
The label should name the maker, packer or distributor and their place of business.

It's a good sign when a small brand is clear about who makes the product and where.

## 5. The packaging matters too
FDA notes that a container can make its contents harmful, such as a lipstick packed in a lead-coated case.
Pick sellers who use clean, sealed, sensible packaging.

## Quick shopping tips
- Ask the seller for the full ingredient list if it isn't in the listing photos.
- Patch-test before using a new balm on your face.
- Store soaps dry, and close jars tightly between uses.

Happy shopping, and jom support small makers wisely!
