Hi everyone!
Nail polish for a weekend event, hair spray for a long day, a spritz of perfume before heading out.
All three can involve **phthalates**. Here's what the US FDA explains.

![Phthalates guide card](/assets/cards/my-phthalates-nail-polish-hair-spray-label-summary.png)

## Product by product
- **Nail polish:** DBP has been used to make polish less brittle.
- **Hair spray:** DMP has been used for a flexible film on hair.
- **Fragrance:** DEP is used as a solvent and fixative.

The FDA also lists **aftershave lotions, cleansers and shampoos** as products that may contain phthalates.

## Which one matters most today
The FDA's 2010 survey found DBP and DMP are **used rarely**.
**DEP** appears to be the only phthalate still commonly used in cosmetics.

## Reading the label
You can spot phthalates by name in the ingredient list.
But **fragrance ingredients don't have to be listed one by one**, so a product may just say "Fragrance."

## FDA's view
The FDA says it has **no safety concerns** with DEP as currently used.
If you want to avoid it anyway, pick products **without "Fragrance" or "Flavor"** listed.

Take care!
