Deepavali is around the corner, and mehndi stalls are popping up at bazaars and malls.
Before you sit down for those pretty patterns, here is one thing worth asking about.

![Plant henna vs black henna card](/assets/cards/my-deepavali-black-henna-ppd-check-summary.png)

## Plant henna and "black henna" are not the same

The henna FDA lists as an approved hair colour comes from the Lawsonia plant.
"Black henna" is a different story.

FDA notes that temporary tattoos marketed as "black henna" contain PPD (paraphenylenediamine).
That same ingredient has been linked more prominently to allergic reactions from hair dyes.

## Why one tattoo can matter later

FDA calls this "cross-sensitization".
People can become allergic to PPD through other exposures, and FDA lists some "black henna" tattoo inks among them.

So a festive tattoo may raise your risk of reacting to hair dye later.
If you colour your hair often, that is worth knowing before you choose a design.

## Hair dye on skin is a misuse

FDA is direct here: hair dyes are not meant for staining your skin.
Artists who use coal-tar hair dyes to colour skin are misusing them, the agency says.

## Quick questions to ask at the stall

Ask what the paste is made of, and whether it contains PPD or any hair dye.
A very dark, jet-black result that sets fast is a good reason to ask twice.

If you have reacted to hair dye before, say so and skip anything you can't confirm.
Itching, redness or swelling after? Contact your healthcare provider.

## For home henna kits

Read the ingredient list on the box.
FDA's hair dye checklist says to make sure ingredients that caused you trouble before, such as PPD, are not present.
