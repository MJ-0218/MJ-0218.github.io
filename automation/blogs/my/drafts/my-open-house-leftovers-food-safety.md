Hello hello!
Open house season means huge pots of curry, briyani and kuih on the table for hours.
Here's how to keep the leftovers safe, using the US CDC's simple food-safety steps.

![Open house leftovers card](/assets/cards/my-open-house-leftovers-food-safety-summary.png)

## The four steps
CDC sums it up as **Clean, Separate, Cook and Chill**.
They're easy to follow even with a full house.

## 1. Clean
Wash hands with soap for **at least 20 seconds** before handling food.
Wash utensils, cutting boards and counters with hot, soapy water after each dish.

## 2. Separate
Keep raw meat and its board away from ready-to-eat food like salads and cut fruit.

## 3. Cook
CDC says a **food thermometer** is the only sure way to tell if food is cooked safely.
When reheating leftovers, aim for **165°F (about 74°C)**.

## 4. Chill
This is where open houses go wrong.
CDC says perishable food shouldn't stay out **more than 2 hours**, or **1 hour above 90°F (about 32°C)**.

On a warm afternoon with the doors open, plan for the 1-hour limit.

## A simple plan for the day
- Put out smaller portions and top up from the kitchen instead of leaving big pots out all day.
- After guests leave, split leftovers into **shallow containers** so they cool fast in the fridge.
- When in doubt about a dish that sat out too long, throw it out.

Happy Deepavali, and jom enjoy those leftovers safely!
