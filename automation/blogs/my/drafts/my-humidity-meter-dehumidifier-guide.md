Hello hello!
Clothes that never quite dry, a wardrobe that smells funny, walls that feel cold and sticky.
Sounds familiar, right?

Before you rush out to buy a dehumidifier, let's check one thing first: how damp is the room, really?

![Humidity check card](/assets/cards/my-humidity-meter-dehumidifier-guide-summary.png)

## Step 1: get a number, not a feeling

A humidity meter, or hygrometer, shows the relative humidity in a room.
The US EPA describes it as a small, inexpensive instrument sold at many hardware stores.

Put it in the bedroom or near the wardrobe for a day or two.
Check it in the morning and again at night, since the reading can change a lot.

## Step 2: what's a good reading?

EPA suggests keeping indoor humidity **below 60%**, and ideally **between 30 and 50 percent**.
Why care? Because, as EPA says, **the key to mould control is moisture control.**

If your room sits above 60% most of the day, that's your sign to act.
In Malaysian weather, a closed room can creep up quickly, especially after a rainy afternoon.

## Step 3: free fixes first

These cost nothing, lah:

- Open the window or switch on the fan when you shower or cook. EPA lists both as ways to bring humidity down.
- Wipe off droplets on windows or pipes. EPA notes that condensation can be a sign of high humidity.
- Don't leave wet towels or a damp laundry pile in the room overnight.

If something gets soaked from a leak, EPA says to dry it **within 24–48 hours** to stop mould from growing.

## Step 4: choosing a dehumidifier

Still above 60% after all that? Then a dehumidifier makes sense.
Here's what to compare on the spec sheet:

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Room size it's rated for | Too small a unit runs all day and still can't keep up |
| Water tank size | A small tank means emptying it more often |
| Continuous drain option | Handy if you run it for long hours |
| Built-in humidity display | Lets you skip buying a separate meter |
| Noise level | Matters if it sits in a bedroom |

Running the aircon on very humid days helps too.
EPA mentions air conditioners and dehumidifiers together as tools to use when needed.

Jom, check that number tonight, and stay dry!
