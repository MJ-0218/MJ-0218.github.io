Hello hello!
Afternoon sun, evening rain, mosquitoes by the drain.
Malaysian weather wants you to wear two products at once, right?

So which one goes on first?
The US CDC actually answers this, so let's sort it out once and for all.

![Sunscreen and repellent order card](/assets/cards/my-sunscreen-then-mosquito-repellent-order-summary.png)

## The order: sunscreen first

CDC's tip is clear: **if you're using sunscreen, apply sunscreen first and insect repellent second.**
Let the sunscreen settle a bit, then put the repellent on top.

Don't put repellent on skin under your clothes, CDC adds.
Only the exposed skin needs it.

## Picking a repellent: check the label

CDC suggests repellents registered with the US EPA that use one of these active ingredients:

| Active ingredient | Note |
|---|---|
| DEET | The classic one |
| Picaridin | Also called icaridin outside the US |
| IR3535 | Common in lotions |
| Oil of lemon eucalyptus (OLE) | Plant-derived, but not for kids under 3 years old |
| PMD | Not for kids under 3 years old |
| 2-undecanone | Plant-derived |

For other countries' products, look for the same active ingredients on the label.
CDC notes it doesn't know how well non-registered "natural" repellents work.

## Kids: a few extra rules

- Don't use OLE or PMD on children under **3 years old**.
- Don't put repellent on a child's hands, eyes, mouth or cuts.
- Spray it on your own hands first, then apply to their face.

## Sunscreen basics, quickly

FDA says to use a **broad spectrum sunscreen with SPF 15 or higher**, and reapply **at least every 2 hours**.
Sweating or swimming? Reapply more often, lah.

There's no such thing as "waterproof" sunscreen, FDA says.
Water-resistant ones must state 40 or 80 minutes on the label.

## Long sleeves still win

CDC also recommends loose, long-sleeved shirts and long pants when mosquitoes are out.
On a hot day, a light, loose fabric keeps you cooler than you'd think.

Jom, sunscreen first, repellent second, and enjoy the evening!
