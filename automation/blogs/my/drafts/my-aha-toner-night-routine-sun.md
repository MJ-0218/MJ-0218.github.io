Hello hello!
Exfoliating toners are a hot add-to-cart item, especially ones with **glycolic** or **lactic acid**.
With our strong sun, though, an AHA needs a little planning. Here's a simple way to fit one in, based on the US FDA's AHA page.

![AHA routine card](/assets/cards/my-aha-toner-night-routine-sun-summary.png)

## What the acid does
AHAs make the outer layer of skin shed.
How strong that is depends on which AHA it is, how much is in the bottle, the pH and the rest of the formula.

Glycolic and lactic acid are the ones you'll see most. Citric acid is an AHA too.

## The sun part, in plain numbers
FDA research found that after four weeks on an AHA cream, skin reddened more easily under UV, by **18 percent**.
Once people stopped, the extra sensitivity was gone within a week.

That's why FDA recommends a "Sunburn Alert" on AHA products: sunscreen, protective clothing and less sun while using it, and **for a week afterwards**.

## A simple weekly plan
- **Night:** use the AHA toner after cleansing, on dry skin.
- **Morning:** sunscreen, every day, even when it's cloudy or you're mostly indoors.
- **Outdoor weekend?** Hat and shade help, too.
- **Stopping?** Keep the sunscreen habit for at least one more week.

## Go gentle
The more serious skin reactions FDA has seen tend to come from products that exfoliate the most, like "skin peelers".
So skip doubling up on several acid products at once.

Stinging or redness that won't calm down? Stop and see a doctor.
