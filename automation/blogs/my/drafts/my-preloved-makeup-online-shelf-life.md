Hello hello!
Preloved makeup bundles and "clearance" lipsticks pop up everywhere, especially before 11.11 and year-end sales.
The prices are tempting. But how fresh is that product, really?

![Preloved makeup card](/assets/cards/my-preloved-makeup-online-shelf-life-summary.png)

## What the FDA warns about
The US FDA says to be wary of products re-sold over the Internet or at flea markets.
Some may be **past their shelf life, already used, diluted, or tampered with**.

They may even be **counterfeit** versions of the product you think you're buying.

## Why "unopened" still matters
Over time, preservatives can break down and let bacteria and fungi grow.
Heat speeds that up, so a lipstick that sat in a hot storeroom or car may have aged faster than its box suggests.

Light and air can also change colour, texture and smell.

## A quick checklist before you pay
- **Ask when it was bought,** and whether it was opened. Get a photo of the batch code.
- **Check the smell and texture** when it arrives. Separated, cracked or sour-smelling? Don't use it.
- **SPF products have a printed expiry date.** In the US they count as drugs and must show one, so look for it.
- **Skip secondhand eye makeup.** FDA notes eye products have shorter shelf lives and may cause serious eye infections.

## Mascara is a hard no
Manufacturers usually recommend tossing mascara two to four months after purchase.
A preloved one has likely used up most of that already.
