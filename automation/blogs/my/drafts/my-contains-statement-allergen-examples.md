Hi everyone!
Picked up some imported US snacks or sauces?
That short "Contains" line on the back is worth a closer look. Here's what it means, according to the US FDA.

![Contains line card](/assets/cards/my-contains-statement-allergen-examples-summary.png)

## Two ways allergens are shown
**1. In brackets** after an ingredient:
- "lecithin (soy)"
- "flour (wheat)"
- "whey (milk)"

**2. In a "Contains" statement** right after or next to the ingredient list:
- "Contains Wheat, Milk, and Soy."

If the ingredient's usual name already shows the source, like buttermilk, that also counts.

## Which allergens?
The US list is milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame.

## What the rule doesn't cover
The FDA label rule covers foods it regulates.
That leaves out **poultry, most meats, certain egg products and most alcoholic drinks**, which other US agencies handle.

## Before you eat
If anyone at home has an allergy, read both the "Contains" line and the full ingredient list.
Labels for other markets may follow different rules.
