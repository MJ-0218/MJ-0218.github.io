Hello Singapore!
Exfoliating toners and serums with **glycolic** or **lactic acid** are everywhere right now.
Before one goes into your basket, here are four label checks based on the US FDA's page on alpha hydroxy acids (AHAs).

![AHA label check card](/assets/cards/sg-aha-glycolic-lactic-sun-check-summary.png)

## Check 1: Is there an AHA at all?
The most common AHAs in cosmetics are **glycolic acid** and **lactic acid**.
Citric acid, hydroxycaprylic acid and hydroxycapric acid also count.

AHAs make the top layer of skin shed.
How much depends on the type, the concentration, the pH and the rest of the formula.

## Check 2: The numbers
An industry safety panel, the Cosmetic Ingredient Review, concluded glycolic and lactic acid products are safe for consumers under conditions that include:

- AHA concentration of **10 percent or less**
- A final product **pH of 3.5 or greater**

Stronger "skin peeler" products are where the more serious reactions tend to show up, FDA says.

## Check 3: The Sunburn Alert line
FDA recommends AHA cosmetics carry a sunburn warning.
It tells you to use sunscreen, wear protective clothing and limit sun exposure while using the product **and for a week afterwards**.

## Check 4: Plan your sun protection
In FDA research, four weeks of an AHA cream raised skin's reddening response to UV by **18 percent**.
The good news: a week after stopping, the difference was gone.

With our year-round sun, that sunscreen step really isn't optional.

## One more thing about salons
Products used only by professionals, such as in salons, don't have to list ingredients in the US.
Getting a peel? It's fair to ask what acid is being used.

Happy shopping, and don't skip that sunscreen!
