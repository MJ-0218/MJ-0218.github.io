Hi everyone. If the sky looked a bit hazy this week, you're not imagining it.
NEA's forecast for **22 October 2026** puts the 24-hr PSI at **101–160, in the Unhealthy range**.
Here's how to read the numbers and plan your day without overthinking it.

![Haze forecast summary card](/assets/cards/sg-haze-psi-forecast-oct-2026-summary.png)

## What NEA is forecasting
For 22 October, the PSI forecast is 101–160 (Unhealthy).
As at 7pm on 21 October, the 1-hr PM2.5 readings were 34–92 µg/m³, across the Normal and Elevated bands.
The 24-hr PSI was 80–123, in the Moderate to Unhealthy range.

Showers are forecast, but NEA also forecasts dry conditions over southern Kalimantan.
With east-southeasterly or southeasterly winds, smoke haze from fires there may be carried towards Singapore.
NEA says some haze may continue on Friday too.

## Two numbers, two jobs
This is the part that trips people up.
NEA uses two different readings for two different purposes:

- **24-hr PSI forecast**: for planning tomorrow's outdoor activities.
- **1-hr PM2.5**: for what you're about to do right now.

So, deciding whether to book an outdoor class tomorrow? Look at the PSI forecast.
About to go for a run this evening? Check the hourly PM2.5 first.

![PM2.5 band table](/assets/cards/sg-haze-psi-forecast-oct-2026-bands.png)

## What to do on an Unhealthy day
NEA's advice for 22 October, by group:

- **Healthy adults**: reduce prolonged or strenuous outdoor exertion. If PM2.5 is Elevated, reduce strenuous outdoor activity.
- **Elderly, pregnant women and children**: minimise prolonged or strenuous outdoor exertion. If PM2.5 is Elevated, avoid strenuous outdoor activity for the next hour.
- **People with chronic lung or heart disease**: avoid prolonged or strenuous outdoor exertion. If PM2.5 is Elevated, avoid strenuous outdoor activity for the next hour.

Working outdoors? The Ministry of Manpower has issued additional guidance for employers to adjust outdoor work based on the hourly PM2.5 readings.

## Where to check, quickly
- NEA's haze microsite: haze.gov.sg
- The myENV app
- MSS for weather: weather.gov.sg

## Small things for a humid, hazy home
- Close windows during Elevated hours, then air the room once readings drop back to Normal.
- Wipe window ledges and fan blades. Haze dust settles faster than you think.
- Run the aircon on recirculate if you have that setting.

Take it easy outdoors this week, and stay well!
