Hello Singapore!
If you've been comparing products marked "phthalate-free," this quick guide is for you.

![Phthalate-free card](/assets/cards/sg-phthalate-free-what-it-means-fragrance-summary.png)

## Why phthalates are used
They're chemicals that make plastics more durable.
In cosmetics, they also act as **solvents and stabilisers in fragrances**.

## What's still in use
Historically, three were common in cosmetics: **DBP** in nail polish, **DMP** in hair spray and **DEP** in fragrance.
The FDA's 2010 survey found DBP and DMP are rarely used now, and **DEP is the only one still commonly used**.

## Why the label may not tell you
Ingredient declarations are required on retail cosmetics, but the **individual components of a fragrance don't have to be listed**.
A product may simply say "Fragrance."

## The FDA's position
The FDA says it **doesn't have safety concerns** with DEP as currently used.
For shoppers who still prefer to avoid it, the FDA's tip is to pick products **without "Fragrance" or "Flavor"** on the ingredient list.

## Quick checklist
- Look for **DBP, DMP or DEP** by name.
- Spot **"Fragrance"** or **"Flavor"** in the list.
- Compare unscented options if you want fewer unknowns.

Happy shopping!
