Hi everyone!
Most of us put on sunscreen. The real question is whether we're putting on enough.
A thin layer that "feels light" often isn't doing the full job.

The US FDA has a handy way to picture the right amount.
Let's go through it, plus the spots that get missed on the way to the MRT.

![Sunscreen amount card](/assets/cards/sg-sunscreen-how-much-to-apply-summary.png)

## The shot-glass rule

FDA says an average-sized adult or child needs **at least 1 ounce of sunscreen**, about the amount that fills a shot glass, to cover the body from head to toe.
That's for the whole body, but the idea is the same for your face: be generous.

If a full-size bottle lasts you months while you use it daily on your face, arms and neck, you're probably using too little.

## Timing: 15 minutes before

FDA's direction is to **apply 15 minutes before sun exposure**.
So put it on before you leave home, not when you're already at the bus stop.

## The spots everyone forgets

FDA lists these as frequently forgotten:

- Ears
- Nose
- Lips (use an SPF lip balm)
- Back of the neck
- Hands
- Tops of feet (hello, sandal tan)
- Along the hairline

## Fair skin, darker skin

FDA notes that fair-skinned people may be more likely to burn than darker-skinned people under the same conditions.
Everyone still benefits from sunscreen, though. Being closer to the equator, as we are, means stronger sun.

## Sprays: not straight on the face

FDA says spray sunscreens should never be applied directly to your face.
Spray onto your hands first, then pat it on.

## Picking a bottle

Look for **broad spectrum** with **SPF 15 or higher**, as FDA recommends.
If you'll use it daily, choose a texture you enjoy wearing. That's the one you'll actually reapply.

Have a bright, burn-free week!
