Hello Singapore!
Deepavali looks often mean bold, smoky eyes, and maybe a lash appointment too.
Here are three checks from the US FDA's eye cosmetic safety page before you go all out.

![Eye-beauty checks card](/assets/cards/sg-kajal-lash-tint-extensions-eye-checks-summary.png)

## Check 1: Is it real kohl, or just the shade name?
Traditional **kohl**, also called al-kahl, kajal or surma, isn't approved for cosmetic use in the US.
FDA explains it is made of heavy metal salts such as **antimony and lead**, and it has been linked to lead poisoning in children.

The good news: some eyeliners use "kohl" only to describe the colour.
If the label lists ingredients, you can check that the colour additives are approved for use around the eye.

So keep any traditional kohl away from kids, and skip products with no ingredient list.

## Check 2: Think twice about DIY lash and brow tints
FDA says permanent eyelash and eyebrow tints and dyes have caused **serious eye injuries, including blindness**.
In the US, the only allowed lash and brow colour is a silver nitrate product for professional use, and it isn't sold directly to consumers.

A box of hair dye is never a lash tint.

## Check 3: Lash extensions need glue
FDA treats false lashes, lash extensions and their adhesives as cosmetics.
Eyelids are delicate, so an allergy or irritation there can be especially troublesome.

Ask your lash artist which adhesive they use, and check its ingredients if you've reacted to anything before.

## If something feels wrong
Stop using any eye product that causes irritation.
Still sore after a day or two? Get it checked by a doctor.

Happy Deepavali, and keep those eyes comfy!
