Hi everyone!
Year-end gatherings are coming, and a fresh hair colour is tempting.
If you're doing it yourself at home, a few minutes of prep can save you from an itchy, painful weekend.

Here's the US FDA's hair dye safety checklist, in plain language.

![Hair dye checklist card](/assets/cards/sg-home-hair-dye-patch-test-checklist-summary.png)

## The patch test: every single time

FDA's advice is to **do a patch test on your skin every time before dyeing your hair**.
Yes, even with a brand you've used for years.

Why? FDA explains that people can become sensitive after repeated use, and formulas can change over time.
So a reaction can happen even if you've dyed your hair before without problems.

Follow the test steps in the box exactly.

## Timing rules

- **Wait at least 14 days** after bleaching, relaxing or perming before you dye, FDA says.
- **Don't scratch or brush your scalp** for three days before dyeing.
- **Skip it** if your scalp is irritated, sunburned or damaged.
- **Use a timer.** Don't leave the dye on longer than the directions say.

## Eyes: hands off

Keep hair dye away from your eyes.
FDA warns that dyeing eyebrows or eyelashes with hair dye can hurt your eyes and may even cause blindness.

## On application day

- Wear gloves.
- Rinse your scalp well with water afterwards.
- Store the box out of children's reach.

## Ingredient to know: PPD

FDA notes that one ingredient, **paraphenylenediamine (PPD)**, has been linked more prominently to allergic reactions.
If you've reacted to a dye before, check the ingredient list for it.

FDA also says temporary "black henna" tattoos can contain PPD and may raise your risk of a hair dye allergy.

## Picking a home kit

Read the full ingredient list before buying, and make sure the box includes clear patch-test directions.
If you've had a reaction before, take the old box to compare ingredients.

Had a reaction? See a doctor first.

Enjoy the new colour, safely!
