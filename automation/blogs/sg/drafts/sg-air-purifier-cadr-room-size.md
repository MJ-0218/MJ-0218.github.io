Hi everyone!
On hazy days, a lot of us start browsing air purifiers.
The spec sheets are full of numbers, so let's focus on the one that matters most for smoke particles: **CADR**.

This guide follows the US EPA's consumer guide to air cleaners.

![Air purifier sizing card](/assets/cards/sg-air-purifier-cadr-room-size-summary.png)

## What CADR means

CADR stands for clean air delivery rate.
EPA says to choose an air purifier with a CADR **large enough for the room** you'll use it in.

The higher the CADR, the more particles it can filter, and the bigger the area it can serve.

## Measure your room

EPA's method: multiply the room's length by its width to get the area.
Then compare that with the maximum room size on the box.

![Minimum CADR by room size](/assets/cards/sg-air-purifier-cadr-room-size-sizing.png)

EPA's chart is based on an **8-foot ceiling**. If your ceiling is higher, go for a higher CADR.
Open-plan living and dining area? Count the whole space.

## Two things to avoid

- **Ozone.** EPA says to avoid air cleaners that intentionally produce ozone, because ozone irritates the lungs.
- **Old filters.** All filters need regular replacement. A dirty, overloaded filter won't work well.

## Other specs worth a look

- **HEPA filter**: EPA notes that many portable air cleaners reach a high CADR with a HEPA filter.
- **Activated carbon filter**: helps with gases and smells, not just particles.
- **Filter price and availability**: you'll be buying replacements.
- **Noise at the speed you'll actually use**, especially for a bedroom.

Keep the windows closed during hazy hours, and breathe easy this week!
