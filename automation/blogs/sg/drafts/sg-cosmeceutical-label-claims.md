Hello Singapore!
"Clinical", "derma", "cosmeceutical"... skincare names are getting very science-y.
Here's what the US FDA says about that last word, and what actually decides how a product is regulated.

![Cosmeceutical card](/assets/cards/sg-cosmeceutical-label-claims-summary.png)

## It's a marketing word
Some brands use "cosmeceutical" to suggest medicinal or drug-like benefits.
But in the US, a product can only be **a drug, a cosmetic, or both**.

## What makes something a drug
Under US law, products meant to deal with disease, or to affect the structure or function of the body, are drugs.
If a product makes claims like that, it's regulated as a drug.

## What makes something a cosmetic
Cosmetics are meant to **beautify, make you more attractive, change your appearance or cleanse**.
They aren't approved by FDA for sale, and they aren't meant to change how the body works.

## How to use this when shopping
- A big promise on a "cosmetic" is worth a second look.
- Focus on the ingredient list and directions, not the buzzwords.
- For a real skin condition, a doctor or pharmacist is the right first stop.

Shop smart, and keep that skin happy!
