Hello Singapore!
We usually think of skincare as a personal thing.
But according to the US FDA, one type of cream can affect everyone at home.

![Mercury household card](/assets/cards/sg-brightening-cream-mercury-family-safety-summary.png)

## The problem with mercury creams
FDA advises avoiding skin creams, beauty soaps and lotions that contain mercury.
They're usually marketed as skin lighteners or anti-aging treatments.

Here's the part many people miss.
FDA says when you use these products, **your family might breathe mercury vapours**, or be exposed through things like **washcloths and towels**.

## Who is most at risk
FDA names a few groups that are **especially vulnerable**:

- Pregnant women
- Nursing babies
- Young children

Mercury is passed into breast milk, so newborns who nurse can be exposed too.
In a small flat where towels, basins and pillowcases are shared, that's worth thinking about.

## Check the label in 10 seconds
Look for these words on the ingredient list:
**mercurous chloride, calomel, mercuric, mercurio, mercury**.

If any of them appear, FDA says to stop using the product immediately.
If there's no ingredient list or no label at all, don't assume it's fine.

## If someone at home has been using one
- Stop using it, and wash hands and skin that touched it.
- Wash shared towels and face cloths separately.
- See a doctor or polyclinic for advice, especially for kids or anyone pregnant.
- Seal the product in a plastic bag or leak-proof container before disposal, and check with your town council or NEA on how to throw it out.

## A simple house rule
Keep everyone's face towels separate, and keep creams with no clear label out of the bathroom altogether.

Take care of yourselves, and your housemates too!
