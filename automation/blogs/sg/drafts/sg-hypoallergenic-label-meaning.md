Hello Singapore!
Sensitive skin shoppers, you've probably reached for a bottle because it said **"hypoallergenic"**.
Here's what that word actually guarantees, according to the US FDA. Spoiler: not much.

![Hypoallergenic label card](/assets/cards/sg-hypoallergenic-label-meaning-summary.png)

## There's no official definition
FDA says there are no federal standards or definitions for "hypoallergenic".
The term means whatever a particular company wants it to mean.

Brands that use it don't have to send FDA any proof, either.
Dermatologists say the word has very little meaning, FDA adds.

## No product is "never" allergenic
FDA put it plainly decades ago: there's no such thing as a "nonallergenic" cosmetic.
In other words, nothing can be guaranteed to never cause a reaction.

## What to check instead
The ingredient list is your friend.
FDA notes it can show whether a "hypoallergenic" product is really any different from a regular one.

If fragrance bothers you, look for **fragrance-free** and read the list carefully.
In the US, fragrance can appear as just one word: "Fragrance".

## A simple routine for new products
- Compare the ingredient lists of two similar products side by side.
- Note any ingredient that bothered you before, and avoid it.
- Unsure about something? Ask the brand.

Shop smart, and take care of that skin!
