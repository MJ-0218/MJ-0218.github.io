Hello Singapore!
Imported vitamins and supplements are easy to buy online.
If a listing shouts "FDA approved," here's what the US FDA actually says.

![Supplement claims card](/assets/cards/sg-supplement-fda-approved-claim-check-summary.png)

## The US FDA doesn't approve supplements
By law, the US FDA does **not** approve dietary supplements for safety and effectiveness.
In fact, many supplements can be marketed **without even notifying** the agency.

The companies are responsible for making sure their products are safe and properly labeled.

## Those "supports your..." lines
Claims about how a nutrient affects the body are called **structure-function claims**. An example FDA gives is "calcium builds strong bones."

On US supplements, these carry a disclaimer that the claim **has not been evaluated by the FDA**.
If you see that line, it's a reminder the claim wasn't reviewed by the agency.

## Food labels too
The US FDA doesn't approve individual food labels either, though statements must be truthful and not misleading.

## Quick checklist
- Treat "FDA approved" on a supplement as a red flag.
- Read the full ingredient and dosage information.
- Ask your doctor or pharmacist before starting something new.

One more thing: this is about the **US** FDA. Some countries have their own agency with a similar name and their own rules, so check your local regulator's system too.

Take care!
