Hi everyone!
That grey patch behind the wardrobe, or the black dots on the bathroom ceiling… we've all been there.
Wiping it off feels good, but it tends to come back.

The US EPA's mould guide explains why, and what to do instead.
Here's a room-by-room version for Singapore flats.

![Mould check card](/assets/cards/sg-hdb-mould-prevention-humidity-summary.png)

## The rule behind everything

EPA says it simply: **the key to mould control is moisture control.**
If there's a leak or a damp corner, fix that first. Otherwise the mould returns.

## Room by room

**Wardrobe**
Leave a small gap between the wardrobe and the wall so air can move.
Don't put away clothes that are still slightly damp from drying indoors.

**Bathroom**
EPA suggests running the bathroom fan or opening the window when you shower.
Wipe the walls and ceiling corners if they stay wet for long.

**Kitchen**
Use the hood or open a window while cooking, as EPA recommends.

**Windows and pipes**
Droplets on the inside of the glass? EPA notes condensation can be a sign of high humidity. Dry it and find the cause.

## What humidity should you aim for?

EPA's target is indoor humidity **below 60%**, ideally between 30 and 50 percent.
A small hygrometer tells you where you stand. On very humid days, aircon or a dehumidifier helps, EPA says.

## After a leak

EPA says to dry water-damaged areas and items **within 24–48 hours** to prevent mould.
So if the upstairs neighbour's pipe drips onto your ceiling, deal with it quickly.

## Clean it yourself, or call someone?

If the mouldy patch is less than about **10 square feet**, roughly 3 ft by 3 ft, EPA says most people can handle it.
Scrub hard surfaces with detergent and water, then dry completely.

Bigger than that, or lots of water damage? Get a professional.

Keep it dry and breezy this week!
