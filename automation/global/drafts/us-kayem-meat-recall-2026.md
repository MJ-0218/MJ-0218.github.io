If you have hot dogs, bacon, sausage or deli meat in the fridge, take a minute to check the label. Massachusetts-based Kayem Foods recalled **1,584,361 pounds** of ready-to-eat meat and poultry products made between March 31 and Oct. 7, 2026, according to the USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS).

![Kayem recall summary](/assets/cards/us-kayem-meat-recall-2026-summary.png)

## What's being recalled

This is a big one. The recall covers hot dogs, sausages, bacon and sliced deli meats, sold under several brand names besides Kayem itself.

Brands named in news reports include:

- Kayem
- Al Fresco
- McKenzie
- Triple M
- Deutschmacher
- Jordan's

![Brands to check](/assets/cards/us-kayem-meat-recall-2026-brands.png)

That list isn't complete. The FSIS recall notice has every product name and UPC, so use it if you're not sure.

## How to check the package

Brand alone won't settle it. Look for the USDA mark of inspection, the small round seal on the package. Recalled products have one of these establishment numbers inside it:

| Establishment number |
|---|
| EST. M/P 7839 |
| EST. P 20935A |
| EST. M 9520 |

If the number matches and the product was made in the March 31 to Oct. 7 window, treat it as recalled.

## Why it happened

It comes down to temperature. A retail customer complained about warm product when it was delivered on Oct. 1. The investigation found the products had been stored in a warehouse cooler that had temperature deviations. Kayem described the cause as a cooling system malfunction at one of its warehouses.

The products went to grocery stores and food service locations across the U.S., and to Hong Kong.

## Has anyone gotten sick?

There have been no confirmed reports of illness so far. The concern is that some of these packages are still sitting in home refrigerators.

## What to do if you have one

Don't eat it. Throw it away, or take it back to the store where you bought it.

Lunch meat and hot dogs tend to get bought in bulk and forgotten in the back of the fridge, so check the freezer too. If you're unsure whether a symptom is food-related, call your doctor.

Check those labels tonight, and stay safe!
