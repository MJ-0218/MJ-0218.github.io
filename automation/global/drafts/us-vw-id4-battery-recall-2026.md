If you drive a Volkswagen ID.4, your car may soon stop charging past **80%**. Volkswagen is recalling about 22,500 ID.4 EVs over a high-voltage battery fire risk. Its first move is an over-the-air update that caps the charge level.

![VW ID.4 recall summary](/assets/cards/us-vw-id4-battery-recall-2026-summary.png)

## What's going on

Car and Driver puts the count at 22,524 vehicles, based on recall documents filed with NHTSA. AZFamily reports more than 22,500.

The problem is fire. According to VW's recall report, as quoted by The Drive, some batteries in this group may overheat under certain charging conditions when the state of charge is over 80%.

Here's the unsettling part: Volkswagen still doesn't know the root cause. That's why the fix for now is a limit, not a repair.

## The 80% cap

Volkswagen will push an over-the-air update that sets the maximum charge to 80%. It's also sending warning stickers telling owners not to charge beyond that point.

This is a temporary measure while VW works on a permanent fix. Until the recall remedy is done, owners are advised to keep charging at 80% or less.

If you'd rather manage charging yourself, keep in mind why the cap exists. The fire risk is tied to charging above 80%, so leaving the limit in place is the safer choice.

## Which ID.4s?

Not every ID.4 is part of this recall, and we couldn't confirm the exact model years in two independent reports. The reliable way to know is your VIN, which ties your specific car to the recall list.

If you've owned your ID.4 for a while, you may also have had earlier recall notices. Check them all at once with the VIN lookup below.

## Does a lower charge cap matter day to day?

For most commuters, probably not much. You lose roughly the top fifth of your battery, which mainly matters on long highway trips. Plan an extra charging stop on road trips until the permanent fix arrives.

## What to do now

1. **Check your VIN** at nhtsa.gov/recalls. You'll find it on the driver's side of the windshield or inside the driver's door.
2. **Charge to 80% or less** until the remedy is done, even if the update hasn't reached your car yet.
3. **Keep an eye out for a letter** from Volkswagen about the recall and the remedy.
4. **Call your VW dealer** if your VIN shows an open recall.

## The bottom line

About 22,500 VW ID.4s are recalled for a battery fire risk with no known root cause yet. Expect a remote 80% charge cap for now. Look up your VIN today instead of waiting for the letter, and keep charging at 80% or below until VW has a real fix.
