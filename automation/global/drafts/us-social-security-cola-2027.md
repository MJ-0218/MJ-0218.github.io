It's official: Social Security and SSI benefits are going up **3.6%** for 2027. The raise reaches about 76 million Americans. If you get Social Security, you'll see it starting with your January 2027 benefits. If you get SSI, the bigger payment arrives a little earlier, on Dec. 31, 2026.

![2027 COLA summary card](/assets/cards/us-social-security-cola-2027-summary.png)

## What 3.6% looks like in dollars

Percentages are hard to picture, so here's a quick example before any Medicare premium is taken out:

- A $1,500 monthly benefit goes up $54, to $1,554.
- A $2,000 monthly benefit goes up $72, to $2,072.

Your exact number depends on your own benefit, and anything deducted from your check (like a Medicare Part B premium) can change the amount that actually lands in your account.

## When the new amount shows up

There are two different start dates, and people mix them up every year:

- **Social Security:** the COLA starts with benefits payable in January 2027. That covers nearly 72 million beneficiaries.
- **SSI:** about 7.3 million recipients get the higher payment starting Dec. 31, 2026.

Some people get both, so you may see the change in two places.

## Where to see your own COLA notice

You don't have to wait for the mail. In late November 2026, SSA will post COLA notices in the Message Center of your my Social Security account. There's one catch: the online notice is available to most beneficiaries who created their account by Nov. 18, 2026. If you've been putting off setting one up, now's a good time.

## Still working? The limits went up too

![Other 2027 Social Security numbers](/assets/cards/us-social-security-cola-2027-numbers.png)

If you collect benefits before full retirement age and still have a paycheck, these matter:

- Under full retirement age all year: the earnings limit rises to $25,560. SSA withholds $1 for every $2 you earn above it.
- Reaching full retirement age in 2027: the limit is $67,920, and SSA withholds $1 for every $3 above it, until the month before you hit full retirement age.
- At full retirement age or older for the whole year: there's no earnings limit.

And for workers, the maximum amount of earnings subject to Social Security tax goes up to $192,300.

## Watch out for COLA scams

Big announcements bring scammers. SSA's own reminder is worth repeating: no government agency will ask for fees by wire transfer or gift card. If a "Social Security" call or text asks for either, hang up.

Enjoy the raise, and keep an eye on your Message Center in late November. Take care!
