If you're 73 or older and have a traditional IRA or 401(k), there's a date to circle: **Dec. 31, 2026**.
That's the deadline for most people to take their **required minimum distribution (RMD)** for this year.

Here's who has to take one, who gets more time and what happens if you miss it.

![RMD deadline card](/assets/cards/us-rmd-deadline-december-31-2026-summary.png)

## What is an RMD?
It's the minimum amount the IRS says you must withdraw from certain retirement accounts **each year**.
You can't keep the money in those accounts forever.

You can always take out **more** than the minimum.
Withdrawals count as taxable income, except any part that was already taxed or qualifies as tax-free.

## Who has to take one
You generally have to start once you reach **age 73**. That covers:

- Traditional IRAs, **SEP IRAs** and **SIMPLE IRAs**
- Workplace plans like **401(k)**, **403(b)** and profit-sharing plans

**Roth IRAs are different.**
You don't have to take withdrawals from a Roth IRA, or from a designated Roth account in a 401(k) or 403(b), while the owner is alive.
Beneficiaries who inherit those accounts do face RMD rules.

## The Dec. 31 deadline
After your first year, every RMD is due by **December 31**.
So if you already started in an earlier year, your 2026 withdrawal needs to be out by **Dec. 31, 2026**.

## The April 1 exception for your first RMD
Your very first RMD gets a little extra time.

- **IRAs:** due by **April 1** of the year after you turn 73.
- **Workplace plans:** generally April 1 after the later of turning 73 or retiring, if your plan lets you wait until retirement.

The still-working delay doesn't apply if you're a **5% owner** of the business that sponsors the plan.
Your plan's own rules can also require you to start at 73 even while you're working.

So if you turn 73 in **2026**, your first IRA RMD can wait until **April 1, 2027**.
The catch: your second RMD is still due by **Dec. 31, 2027**, so you'd take **two** withdrawals in 2027.

## How the amount is figured
Each account's RMD is generally its **prior Dec. 31 balance** divided by a **life expectancy factor** from IRS Publication 590-B.

Have several IRAs?
You calculate the RMD for each one separately, but you can withdraw the **total from one or more** of them.

An IRA RMD and a 401(k) RMD are separate, though.
Taking money from your IRA doesn't cover what's due from your workplace plan.

## What if you miss it?
The amount you didn't withdraw may face a **25% excise tax**.
That drops to **10%** if you correct it within **two years**.

You report it on **Form 5329** with your federal tax return.

## Quick to-do list
- Check every traditional IRA and workplace plan you have.
- Ask your plan or IRA custodian for your 2026 RMD amount.
- If you turned 73 this year, decide whether to take your first RMD now or by April 1, 2027.

Here's to a smooth, penalty-free year-end!
