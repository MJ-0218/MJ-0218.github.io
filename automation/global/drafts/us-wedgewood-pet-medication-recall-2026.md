If your pet takes a compounded medication, this one is worth a few minutes.
**Wedgewood Pharmacy** is recalling certain compounded veterinary medications because some may be weaker or stronger than they should be.

Here's how to find out if yours is affected.

![Wedgewood recall card](/assets/cards/us-wedgewood-pet-medication-recall-2026-summary.png)

## What's going on

Wedgewood found problems with the reliability of some testing data from its own lab.
That means some products could be **subpotent or superpotent**.

Low potency can mean the treatment doesn't work. High potency can mean toxicity or an overdose.

## Which products

The recall covers certain compounded medications shipped **between Feb. 18, 2026 and Oct. 6, 2026** to vet practices, pharmacies and other customers across the U.S.

They come in many forms: powders, pastes, oral suspensions, injectables, eye ointments and solutions, ear solutions, topical sprays and gels.
They're for companion animals, horses, and exotic, zoo, wildlife and aquatic animals.

The full list of products, lot numbers and expiration dates is on Wedgewood's recall lookup page: **wedgewood.com/recall-lookup**.

## Have there been problems?

Wedgewood says it has received **98 complaints**, and **7** of them are serious.

## What pet owners should do

- **Stop using** the affected product.
- **Keep it**, don't throw it away yet.
- Call Wedgewood's recall line at **866-562-5931**: Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. ET, and Saturday 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. ET.

Talk to your veterinarian if you're worried about your pet's health, or before stopping a medication your pet depends on.
The recall line can also help find alternative treatments.

Refunds or, where possible, replacements are being arranged.

## Report a reaction

If your pet had a bad reaction, you can report it to the FDA at **1-888-FDA-VETS**.

Give your pet an extra scratch for us, and check that label today.
