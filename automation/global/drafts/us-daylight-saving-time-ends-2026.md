Daylight saving time ends on **Sunday, Nov. 1, 2026, at 2 a.m.** local time. At that moment clocks jump back to 1 a.m., so you get an extra hour that night. That's 13 days from today.

![Daylight saving time ends Nov. 1 card](/assets/cards/us-daylight-saving-time-ends-2026-summary.png)

## What actually happens at 2 a.m.

The hour from 1 a.m. to 2 a.m. happens twice. Your phone and most computers will handle it on their own. The usual suspects that won't: the microwave, the oven, older car dashboards and some wall clocks.

The rule behind the date is simple. Daylight saving time ends at 2 a.m. on the first Sunday of November and starts again at 2 a.m. on the second Sunday of March. During 2026 it began on March 8. Those rules have been in place since 2007, when they changed for the first time in more than 20 years.

## Who doesn't change their clocks?

Not everyone in the U.S. switches. Daylight saving time isn't observed in:

- Hawaii
- Arizona, with one exception: the Navajo Indian Reservation does observe it
- Puerto Rico, Guam, American Samoa and the Virgin Islands

![Where clocks stay put](/assets/cards/us-daylight-saving-time-ends-2026-skip.png)

If you live in Arizona and have a clock that sets itself by radio signal, NIST suggests turning off its automatic DST setting so it doesn't jump on its own.

## Quick answers

**Do we gain or lose an hour?** You gain one. In November the clock goes back, so Saturday night is an hour longer.

**Does the whole country change at the same moment?** No. The switch happens at 2 a.m. *local* time, so the East Coast changes first and each time zone follows an hour later.

**Is Nov. 1 a weekend?** Yes, it's a Sunday. Both the fall and spring changes always land on a Sunday at 2 a.m.

## When does it start again?

Clocks spring forward on the second Sunday of March. In 2027 that's March 14, at 2 a.m. local time, when the time skips straight to 3 a.m.

## A few small things worth doing this week

Here's the short list:

1. Check any device that doesn't update itself, like car clocks or appliance timers.
2. If you take medication on a strict schedule, ask your pharmacist how to handle the shift.
3. Kids and pets don't read calendars. Shifting bedtime a little earlier or later over a few nights can make Monday easier.

One fun fact before you go: the U.S. first made daylight saving time official in 1918. And for the record, the time change is regulated by the U.S. Department of Transportation, not NIST, even though NIST keeps the nation's official time.

Enjoy the extra hour of sleep, and have a great week!
