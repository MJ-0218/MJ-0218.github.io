Heating bills are about to climb again as the cold sets in.
If money is tight, the federal **Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP)** is the first place to look.

Here's what it can cover, who can qualify and how to apply without getting scammed.

![LIHEAP winter help card](/assets/cards/us-liheap-heating-help-how-to-apply-winter-2026-summary.png)

## What LIHEAP can pay for
LIHEAP is federally funded help with home energy costs.
According to HHS, it can help with:

- **Home heating bills** during the winter
- **Preventing energy shutoffs** and **reconnecting service** that was cut off
- **Energy crises**, like running out of fuel in a cold snap
- **Weatherization** and **minor energy-related home repairs**, including repairing or replacing heating equipment

It also helps with cooling in the summer, so it's worth remembering year-round.

## Who qualifies: the income rule
Eligibility is based on your **household income**, and every state sets its own exact limits.

Federal rules put a ceiling on those limits.
A state can make you eligible on income alone if you earn no more than the **greater of**:

- **150% of the federal poverty guidelines** for your state, or
- **60% of your state's median income**

States also can't turn you away on income alone if you're below **110% of the poverty guidelines**.
They can still give priority to households with the highest energy costs or needs.

In some states, the 60% state median income number is the higher one.
That means more middle-income families qualify than people expect, so check your state's chart before you rule yourself out.

## How to apply
LIHEAP money doesn't come straight from Washington.
HHS sends it to states, territories and tribes, and they run the local programs.

1. **Check your eligibility** with your state's income limits.
2. **Find your state or territory's LIHEAP office.**
3. **Apply.** Some offices take applications online. Others ask you to apply in person.

Not sure where your office is?
Call the **National Energy Assistance Referral (NEAR) line at 1-866-674-6327** and they'll point you to the right place.

## Bonus: weatherization help
The **Weatherization Assistance Program (WAP)** is a separate program for home improvements that cut energy use.
It's also income-based, and you may qualify if you already get benefits like **TANF** or **SSI**.

Contact your state's WAP office to see if it's open in your area.

## Power already cut off?
Each state has its own rules on when utilities can and can't disconnect electricity or gas for nonpayment.
Those rules can depend on the weather, your age, a disability and your utility provider.

Look up your state's policy and call your utility or local LIHEAP office right away.

## Watch out for the LIHEAP grant scam
This one matters.
HHS says **LIHEAP does not give direct grants to individuals** and **does not charge a fee** for a benefit.

If someone calls, texts or messages you offering a "LIHEAP grant" or asks for a fee, it's a scam.
Report it to the **HHS Fraud Hotline at 1-800-447-8477**.

## Quick takeaway
Start with your state's LIHEAP office or the free 1-866-674-6327 line.
Never pay anyone to apply, and never share bank details with a caller offering a "grant."

Stay warm out there!
