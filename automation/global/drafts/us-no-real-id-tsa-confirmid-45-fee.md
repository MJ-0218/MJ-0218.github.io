Holiday travel season is close, and "REAL ID" searches are climbing again.
If your driver's license isn't REAL ID compliant, here's what TSA's own pages say right now.

![REAL ID card](/assets/cards/us-no-real-id-tsa-confirmid-45-fee-summary.png)

## The basic rule

TSA says that since **May 2025**, travelers must be REAL ID compliant to board domestic flights and enter certain federal facilities.
Adults **18 and older** must show valid ID at the airport checkpoint.

## IDs that still work

A REAL ID license isn't the only option. TSA's list of acceptable IDs includes:

- U.S. passport or passport card
- DHS trusted traveler cards (Global Entry, NEXUS, SENTRI, FAST)
- Permanent resident card
- U.S. Department of Defense ID
- Foreign government-issued passport
- Certain state mobile driver's licenses approved for federal use

TSA says the list can change without notice, so check it again before you fly.

## No acceptable ID? The $45 option

Starting **Feb. 1, 2026**, if you can't show an acceptable ID at the checkpoint, you can pay a **$45 fee** to use **TSA ConfirmID**.
TSA then tries to verify your identity so you can go through screening.

TSA suggests paying the fee online before you head to the airport.

## Expired ID?

TSA currently accepts expired ID **up to two years after expiration** for the IDs on its list.

## Traveling with kids

TSA does not require children **under 18** to show identification when traveling within the U.S.

## Getting a REAL ID

Your state DMV issues REAL ID licenses.
TSA's REAL ID page lets you pick your state and plan a DMV visit.

Plan ahead, and safe travels this holiday season!
