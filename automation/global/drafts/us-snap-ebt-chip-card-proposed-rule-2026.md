If you use SNAP, your EBT card may look different in a few years.
USDA has proposed a rule to make EBT cards work more like the debit card in your wallet.

Important: this is a **proposed rule, not a final one**. Nothing changes for your card today, and the details could still shift.

Here's what's in it, in plain English.

![SNAP EBT card security summary](/assets/cards/us-snap-ebt-chip-card-proposed-rule-2026-summary.png)

## Why USDA wants new cards
Most EBT cards still use only a magnetic stripe.
Thieves can copy that stripe with a skimming device and drain benefits from the account.

The scale is big.
As of early fiscal 2025, states had approved **753,929 claims** to replace stolen SNAP benefits, worth about **$360 million**.

And the federal money for replacing stolen benefits ran out on Dec. 20, 2024.
So stopping theft before it happens matters more now.
If your benefits get skimmed today, getting them back isn't guaranteed the way it was.

## What would change
Under the proposal, states would issue EBT cards with **chip and tap-to-pay (NFC)** technology.
USDA proposes giving states **two years after a final rule** to make the switch.

Some states are already ahead.
As of August 2026, seven states had issued chip EBT cards.

States would also have to tell households and stores before the switch.
So you shouldn't wake up one day to a card that suddenly doesn't work.

![What the SNAP EBT proposal would change](/assets/cards/us-snap-ebt-chip-card-proposed-rule-2026-changes.png)

## New security tools for your account
The proposal also lists tools states would have to offer:

- **Account blocks** you can switch on or off, such as blocking out-of-state or online purchases
- **Alerts by text or email** when your card is used
- **A card security code** printed on the card
- **Rules against easy PINs**, like 1212 or PINs starting with 19 or 20

That last one surprises people.
A PIN that starts with 19 or 20 often looks like a birth year, and thieves guess those first.

## What you can do now
- Change your PIN if it's a simple pattern or a birth year.
- Check your state's EBT app or website. Some states already let you lock your card.
- Look at card readers before you swipe. Loose or bulky parts can be a skimmer.
- Check your balance often, so you'll notice strange charges quickly.
- Don't share your card number or PIN by phone or text. Your state won't ask for your PIN that way.

## Want to weigh in?
Anyone can comment on the proposal at regulations.gov.
Comments are due by **Dec. 22, 2026**. USDA will review them before writing a final rule.

It's still a proposal, so watch for a final rule before anything changes.
Until then, the best protection is still a strong PIN and a quick look at the card reader.

Stay safe, and keep that PIN tricky.
